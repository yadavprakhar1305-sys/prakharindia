Stamp Duty & Property Registration Charges in Uttar Pradesh (2026)

Stamp duty is the single largest transaction cost most buyers underestimate. In Uttar Pradesh, the total outgo at registration commonly lands between 6% and 8% of the property's assessable value — which on a ₹50 lakh purchase is ₹3–4 lakh paid in one day, over and above the down payment. Knowing how the number is built, and what counts as assessable value, can save both money and a failed registration attempt.

This guide explains the structure, the documents, the process, and the mistakes that cause buyers to pay more than they should.

Two separate charges apply. Stamp duty is a state tax on the instrument of transfer. Registration fee is charged for recording that instrument in the government's registry. Both are calculated on the higher of the actual consideration stated in the sale deed and the circle rate — the minimum valuation notified by the state for that locality.

The stamp duty rate depends mainly on the buyer's category. Uttar Pradesh has historically offered a concession to female buyers, and the exact percentages are revised from time to time through notifications. Rather than rely on a rate you read online, confirm the current rate at the Sub-Registrar office or on the IGRSUP portal before you finalise your budget. As a planning assumption, most male buyers should budget in the 6.5–7.5% range including registration, and female buyers somewhat lower.

Important: circle rates and stamp duty percentages are revised by the state government. The figures in this article are planning estimates for 2026, not a substitute for the rate applicable on your registration date. Always confirm at the Sub-Registrar office or on the official IGRSUP portal.

What counts as assessable value

If you pay ₹60 lakh for a property whose circle rate valuation is ₹52 lakh, stamp duty is calculated on ₹60 lakh. If you pay ₹45 lakh for a property valued at ₹52 lakh on the circle rate, stamp duty is calculated on ₹52 lakh. This is why under-reporting the sale price does not reduce the tax — it only creates a legal exposure, because the difference can later be treated as unaccounted money.

Other amounts may also be added to the assessable value depending on how the deed is drafted, including charges for parking, amenities or maintenance paid to the seller. Ask the deed writer to show you the full computation before the appointment.

A worked example

Suppose a male buyer purchases a flat for ₹50 lakh in a locality where the circle rate valuation is ₹48 lakh. Stamp duty is computed on ₹50 lakh. At an assumed combined rate of about 7%, the payable amount is roughly ₹3.5 lakh. Add legal fees, any broker commission, bank processing charges and the cost of mutation, and the total transaction cost moves closer to 8% of the purchase price.

The registration process

  1. Verify the title. A lawyer should check the sale deed chain, the khatauni, the encumbrance certificate and the layout approval before any drafting begins.
  2. Obtain the e-stamp. Stamp duty is paid electronically through the IGRSUP portal or an authorised bank, and the e-stamp certificate is generated against the parties' names.
  3. Draft the sale deed. The deed should describe the property precisely, state the consideration, list the encumbrances and record the possession date.
  4. Book the appointment. Both buyer and seller, along with two witnesses, attend the Sub-Registrar office with originals and photocopies.
  5. Biometric verification and photographs. These are captured at the registry.
  6. Pay the registration fee. Payment is usually made electronically at the office or through the portal.
  7. Collect the registered deed. Verify that the registration number, the parties' names and the property description are correct before you leave.
  8. Complete mutation. Apply for dakhil kharij so the revenue record reflects the new owner.

Documents to carry

  • Original and photocopies of the sale deed
  • Previous title deeds and the chain of ownership
  • PAN card, Aadhaar card and photographs of both parties
  • E-stamp certificate
  • Property tax receipts and latest utility bills
  • No-objection certificate from the society or builder, where applicable
  • Proof of payment — bank statements or demand drafts
  • Two witnesses with identity proof

Costs beyond stamp duty

Registration fee is usually about 1% of the assessable value, subject to the state's current rules. Legal fees for title verification and deed drafting vary with the property's complexity. Mutation charges are modest but the process takes time. Bank charges apply if you are financing the purchase — processing fee, valuation fee and stamp duty on the loan agreement. Brokerage is negotiable and should be agreed in writing before the deal.

Five mistakes that cost buyers money

  • Understating the sale price. Duty is charged on the circle rate if it is higher, so the saving is illusory and the legal risk is real.
  • Ignoring the female-buyer concession. Where the purchaser is a woman, the lower rate can be substantial. Structure ownership correctly from the start rather than attempting a correction later.
  • Skipping mutation. Registration without dakhil kharij leaves the revenue record in the seller's name and creates problems at resale or when applying for a loan.
  • Not checking dues. Unpaid property tax, electricity bills and society maintenance can become the buyer's problem if the deed does not settle them.
  • Using an unverified deed writer. A poorly drafted deed can misdescribe the property, omit an encumbrance, or fail to record the possession date. Use a lawyer who will stand behind the document.

Joint ownership and the female concession

Many families add a wife, mother or daughter as a co-owner to take advantage of the lower stamp duty rate applicable to female purchasers. The rules on how the concession applies to joint ownership are specific, and they change. Confirm the treatment with the Sub-Registrar before drafting, and make sure the ownership shares in the deed reflect the family's actual intention — not just the tax calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions: UP Property Registration

What are the registration charges for residential plots in UP?

Registration charges for plots in Uttar Pradesh are calculated based on either the actual purchase price or the notified Circle Rate (whichever is higher). Stamp duty is 7% for male buyers and 6% for female buyers (with ₹10,000 concession), accompanied by a 1% mandatory registration fee. Explore our guide on verified plots in Mirzapur and UP.

How does joint registry work for husband and wife in UP?

When property is registered jointly in the names of husband and wife, the stamp duty rate is effectively 6.5% plus the 1% registration fee (total 7.5%). The female concession applies to her fractional share of ownership.

What bank documents are required if taking a home loan for registration?

If financing your purchase through a bank or housing finance company, review our complete checklist on home loan documents required for plot and house construction in UP, including title search report, 13-year non-encumbrance certificate, and sanctioned layout.

How to pay stamp duty online in Uttar Pradesh?

Online stamp duty in UP is paid through the official IGRSUP (Integrated Grievance and Revenue System Uttar Pradesh) portal or Stock Holding Corporation of India (SHCIL) e-stamping platform. Once generated, the e-stamp certificate is attached to the sale deed for appointment at the Sub-Registrar office.
