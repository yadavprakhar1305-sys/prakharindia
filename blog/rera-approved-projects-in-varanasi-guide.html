RERA Approved Projects & Villa Buying Guide in Varanasi (2026)

Uttar Pradesh was among the first states to operationalise the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, and UP RERA now covers a large share of the state's organized residential and commercial supply. For a buyer in Varanasi, the practical value of RERA is not the certificate on the brochure — it is the information the promoter is legally required to disclose, and the remedies available if the project does not deliver what was promised.

This guide explains what RERA registration actually guarantees, how to verify a project, which local approvals sit alongside it, and what to do when something goes wrong.

What UP RERA registration means

A registered project has disclosed its land title, the sanctioned layout, the number of units, the common areas, the construction timeline and the promoter's background. The promoter must update the project's progress quarterly and must keep 70% of the amounts collected from buyers in a designated project account, to be used only for that project's construction and land cost.

Registration also triggers buyer protections: the promoter cannot unilaterally change the sanctioned plan without the consent of a specified proportion of buyers, cannot delay possession beyond the date declared without attracting interest liability, and cannot transfer or mortgage the project without the required approvals.

How to verify a project

  1. Go to the official UP RERA website and open the registered projects section.
  2. Search by project name, promoter name or district.
  3. Open the project page and check the registration number and its validity.
  4. Review the declared completion date, the number of units and the sanctioned layout.
  5. Check the quarterly progress reports and the promoter's compliance history.
  6. Cross-check the registration number printed in the brochure or agreement against the website.

If the number does not appear, or appears against a different project or promoter, stop. A common tactic is to quote a registration number from one phase of a project while selling an unregistered later phase.

Check the phase, not just the project. Large developments are often registered in phases. The tower you are buying into may sit in a phase that is not yet registered, even though the overall project is. Match the tower, the unit count and the declared completion date to the registration entry.

The local approvals that sit alongside RERA

RERA does not replace planning permission. In Varanasi, layouts and building plans in the development area are sanctioned by the Varanasi Development Authority, while Nagar Nigam Varanasi handles municipal services and sanctioned construction within city limits. Projects near protected monuments or the riverfront may also need heritage clearance.

A RERA-registered project should still be able to show you the sanctioned plan, the layout approval and the commencement certificate. If the promoter has RERA registration but cannot produce the local sanction, the registration is not doing the work you think it is.

Varanasi's micro-markets and what to check in each

Sigra, Mahmoorganj and Bhelupur. Established residential and commercial areas with strong rental demand. Supply is mostly redevelopment and small apartment buildings. Check parking, setback compliance and the age of the surrounding infrastructure.

Lanka and BHU belt. Student and faculty demand supports consistent rentals. Verify the permitted use and the actual occupancy norms, and expect higher wear and tear.

Sarnath and the airport road. Growing hotel and residential supply driven by tourism and the airport. Check the road access and the status of the layout, because some colonies here are still being regularized.

Ramnagar and the Ring Road corridor. More affordable plotted and apartment supply. Confirm the bridge connectivity and the actual travel time to the city, and check the flood level if the plot is close to the river.

Heritage core and ghats. Highly sensitive. Height, façade, access and monument proximity rules apply, and clearances can take time. Do not buy on the assumption that a guest-house conversion will be approved.

Documents to read before signing

  • The agreement for sale, in the format prescribed under RERA
  • The sanctioned layout and floor plans, with the carpet area of your unit stated
  • The declaration of common areas and the parking allocation
  • The construction timeline and the grace period, if any
  • The payment schedule and the penalties for delay by either side
  • The promoter's title documents and the encumbrance certificate
  • The project's RERA registration number and quarterly reports

Red flags

  • No RERA number, or a number that does not match the project on the portal
  • Booking amounts demanded in cash or to a personal account rather than the project account
  • A payment plan that front-loads most of the cost before construction begins
  • Refusal to share the sanctioned plan or the title documents
  • Possession dates that are earlier than the construction progress could possibly support
  • Changes to the layout or unit size after booking, without written consent

If something goes wrong

UP RERA provides a complaint mechanism for delays, defects, unauthorized changes and non-compliance with the agreement. Complaints can be filed online with the authority, and the authority can direct the promoter to comply, pay interest for delay, or refund the amount with interest in specified circumstances. Keep every receipt, the agreement, the brochures and the correspondence. Buyers who have documented the transaction clearly are in a much stronger position than those relying on verbal assurances.

How do I check if a plot in Varanasi is genuinely RERA approved?

Always request the developer's 14-digit UP RERA registration number (e.g., UPRERAPRJ...). Verify it on up-rera.in under the Registered Projects tab.
