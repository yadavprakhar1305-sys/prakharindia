Labour Contractor Compliance in India: EPF, ESI & New Labour Codes 2026

Legal & Compliance Updated: September 2026 By Compliance Cell, Prakhar India

Hiring contract labour for factories, infrastructure sites, warehouses, or construction projects comes with strict legal responsibilities in India. Under the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 and the incoming 4 Labour Codes (Wages, Social Security, Industrial Relations, and Occupational Safety), companies cannot afford non-compliant staffing vendors.

At Prakhar India, our core operational promise is 100% statutory compliance. In this guide, we break down what corporate buyers, plant managers, and builders must verify before signing a labour contracting agreement.

1. The Four Non-Negotiable Compliance Pillars

A. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF & MP Act, 1952)

Every worker employed through a contractor earning basic wages up to ₹15,000/month must be enrolled in EPF. The contractor must generate monthly Electronic Challan cum Return (ECR) statements proving deposit of both employee (12%) and employer (12%) contributions.

B. Employees' State Insurance (ESIC Act, 1948)

Applicable to all units employing 10 or more workers where wages do not exceed ₹21,000/month. ESI provides comprehensive medical care and cash benefits for workplace injuries. Always demand monthly ESI contribution receipts tagged to individual IP numbers.

C. UP State Minimum Wages Act Compliance

Uttar Pradesh revises its minimum wage structure bi-annually (April and October) based on the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Paying below official rates is a cognizable offense that can result in immediate site closure and severe fiscal penalties.

D. CLRA Registration & Labour License

If you engage 20+ contract workers, the principal employer must hold Form-I Registration, and the contractor must possess a valid Form-VI Labour License issued by the UP Labour Commissioner.

⚠️ Critical Warning for Principal Employers: Section 21(4) of the CLRA Act explicitly states that if the contractor fails to pay wages or deposit statutory dues, the principal employer is liable to pay the full amount out of pocket.

2. Documents You Must Demand Every Month

Before releasing your contractor's monthly billing invoice, insist on receiving:

  • Monthly Wage Register (Form XVII / Muster Roll) signed by workers.
  • EPF Monthly ECR Challan alongside Transaction Confirmation Slip (TRRN).
  • ESIC Monthly Contribution Summary Sheet and Challan payment receipt.
  • Proof of direct bank transfer (NEFT/RTGS) of worker wages.
  • Valid Workmen Compensation (WC) Insurance policy copy.
