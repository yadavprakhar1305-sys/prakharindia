HOME LOAN GUIDE

Home Loan & Plot Construction Loan Documents Checklist in UP

A home loan rejection usually has little to do with the borrower's income and everything to do with documentation. Banks and housing finance companies approve a loan only when three separate things line up: the borrower can service the EMI, the property is legally clear, and the construction being funded is technically sound. Each of those is proved with paper.

This guide lists the documents lenders in Uttar Pradesh commonly ask for, explains the difference between a plot loan, a home loan and a composite construction loan, and flags the mistakes that slow down or sink an application.

First, understand what the lender is testing

Repayment capacity is judged from income, existing EMIs and credit history. Most lenders cap total EMIs at roughly 40–55% of net monthly income, depending on the profile. A credit score in the high 700s or above gets the smoothest processing; a score below 700 usually means a higher rate, a lower loan-to-value ratio, or a rejection.

Legal title is judged from the sale deed, the chain of ownership and the revenue records. Technical feasibility is judged from the approved plan, the estimate and a site inspection. A file that is strong on income but weak on title will still be declined.

KYC and identity documents

  • Aadhaar card and PAN card
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Address proof — utility bill, rent agreement or bank statement
  • Signature verification and, where applicable, a power-of-attorney if the applicant is overseas

Make sure the name, address and date of birth match exactly across documents. A mismatch between the Aadhaar and PAN spelling is one of the most common causes of avoidable delay.

Income documents — salaried applicants

  • Last three months' salary slips
  • Last six months' bank statements showing salary credits
  • Form 16 for the last two years
  • Employer identity card and, in some cases, an employment confirmation letter
  • Details of any existing loans, credit cards and their EMIs

Income documents — self-employed applicants

  • Income tax returns with computation for the last three years
  • Audited financial statements and profit-and-loss accounts
  • Business proof — GST registration, trade licence, or firm registration
  • Last twelve months' bank statements for the business and personal accounts
  • Advance tax challans and TDS certificates

Lenders typically average the last three years' declared income, so a sharp drop in the most recent year can reduce the eligible loan amount even if the business is healthy.

Property documents for a plot purchase

  • Registered sale deed or allotment letter
  • Chain of title documents going back at least 13 years, sometimes 30
  • Current khatauni, khasra and mutation record
  • Non-encumbrance certificate for the relevant period
  • Approved layout plan and the sanction letter from the development authority
  • Land-use certificate confirming residential use
  • Property tax receipts and latest utility bills
  • No-objection certificate from the society or builder, if applicable

Additional documents for construction on your own land

  • Approved architectural and structural drawings from the local authority
  • Itemised engineer's construction cost estimate
  • Contractor agreement and the contractor's registration details
  • Proof of own contribution — bank statements or fixed deposits earmarked for the build
  • Soil test report, where the lender or structure requires it
  • Approval for water and electricity connections

Composite loan advantage: if you already own the plot, a composite plot-plus-construction loan can fund both the land and the building under one sanction. It often works out cheaper than a separate plot loan followed by a fresh home loan, because you avoid a second set of processing fees and a second legal valuation.

How construction-linked disbursement works

For a self-built house, the lender does not release the full amount upfront. It releases funds in stages, usually after a site inspection confirms that the previous stage is complete. A typical sequence is: foundation and plinth, roof slab, brickwork and plastering, flooring and finishing. Each stage has a percentage attached to it in the sanction letter.

This is why the construction estimate matters. If the estimate is understated, the sanctioned amount will not cover the actual build, and you will have to fund the gap from savings. Get a realistic, itemised estimate — not a one-line per-square-foot figure — and keep a contingency of at least 10%.

Why applications get rejected or delayed

  • Title defects. Missing links in the chain, undivided ancestral property, or a sale executed by someone without clear authority.
  • Unauthorized construction. Existing structure that does not match the sanctioned plan.
  • Agricultural land without conversion. Lenders generally will not finance construction on land still recorded as agricultural.
  • Low credit score or high existing EMI burden. Adding a co-applicant with independent income can improve eligibility.
  • Cash transactions. Large cash deposits or withdrawals in the bank statement raise red flags and require explanation.
  • Incomplete file. A single missing document can stall processing for weeks, so submit a complete set at one time.

Costs beyond the EMI

Budget for the processing fee, legal and technical valuation charges, stamp duty on the loan agreement, and any property insurance the lender requires. In Uttar Pradesh, stamp duty and registration on the purchase itself are separate and usually fall in the 6–8% range depending on the buyer's category and the property's circle rate. A simple document checklist

Keep three folders: identity and income, property legal, and construction technical. Scan every document at 300 dpi, name the files clearly, and keep originals ready for verification. Ask the lender for a written list of requirements at the start rather than supplying documents piecemeal, and get the sanction letter — including the interest rate, tenure and disbursement schedule — before paying any booking amount to a seller.
