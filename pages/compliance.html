Compliance in Practice

What verification looks like before handover

Compliance is not a certificate on a wall. On a live site it is a sequence of checks: the land title is clean before sale, the drawing is approved before excavation, the reinforcement is inspected before concrete, and the completion file is complete before the keys change hands. Our compliance process is built around that sequence rather than around a single approval.

For plotted developments and apartment projects, eligible projects are registered under UP-RERA with disclosure of the promoter, project timeline, sanctioned plans and common-area commitments. Buyers can verify the registration number on the UP-RERA portal before paying a booking amount, and we provide the registration details in the booking kit rather than on request alone.

Structural work follows IS 456:2000 for plain and reinforced concrete and IS 1893 for earthquake-resistant design, with the seismic zone treated as a design input rather than a footnote. Soil bearing capacity is tested before foundations are sized. Concrete cubes are cast at fixed intervals and tested at 7 and 28 days, and steel is checked against mill test certificates for grade and elongation before it reaches the slab.

Documents a buyer should ask for

Any serious developer or contractor should be able to produce these without delay:

  • RERA registration and the project disclosure for registered developments.
  • Sanctioned building plan, layout and setback drawings.
  • Title chain, mutation records and encumbrance certificate.
  • Soil test report and structural design calculations.
  • Concrete cube results and steel mill test certificates.
  • Completion or occupancy certificate where the local body requires one.
  • Payment schedule, GST invoices and possession letter.

Labour and site safety

Contract labour is engaged with the statutory registrations and insurance that apply to the project, and sites run inductions, PPE checks, scaffolding inspections, electrical isolation and debris management. Inspection records are kept available to buyers and institutional clients on request. If something on site does not match the approved drawing, we stop the stage and resolve it in writing before work continues.
